How this model is calculated

The vitreous cavity is a sphere. Axial length, when chosen, sets volume by V = (AL³ π / 6) · (0.76 + 0.012 (AL − 24)). Air, SF₆, and C₃F₈ share one static gas model: occupying volume is the given mL (no Boyle / IOP compression and no expansion over time); the bubble is axisymmetric about gravity and sits at the zenith. One Young–Laplace solve with gravity is used at every fill: σ κ = ΔP₀ + Δρ g z, with a Young angle through the cavity liquid. Gas–liquid σ and θ come from that fluid: vitreous ~61 mN/m and 39° (Ross 2010; PLOS 2020; air–retina CFD), aqueous ~65 mN/m and 30°, BSS ~72 mN/m and 30°. Small Bond number keeps the bubble round; large Bond number flattens the underside. Gases are isoexpansile (air; 20% SF₆; 14% C₃F₈). Mixture density is ρ = f ρ_pure + (1−f) ρ_air; Δρ = 1000 − ρ. Tamponade is contact if the break’s polar angle from zenith is ≤ β. Force on gas-contact retina is ΔP = σ κ₀ + Δρ g (z − z_apex); it is zero on the fluid side.

Silicone oil uses the same cavity and YL solver with σ ≈ 32–40 mN/m (lower in vitreous than BSS), Δρ ≈ 30 kg/m³, and a 25° Young angle, so it stays rounded. 1000 cs and 5000 cs differ mainly in viscosity; the static shape is almost the same. The macula is a 2.75 mm-radius disk around the fovea. Clock hours are as you face the eye, like a clock: 12 up, 3 right (patient-left), 9 left (patient-right). The macula is at the back of the globe. The optic disc is 4.5 mm nasal to the fovea (toward 3 o’clock in OD, toward 9 o’clock in OS). Vascular arcades leave the disc and pass 4 mm above and below the fovea. The ora serrata is a circle on the sphere, tilted with the nerve: at 24 mm axial length the nasal point is 17.5 mm from the anterior apex and the temporal point is 18.75 mm; each millimetre of extra axial length adds 0.16 mm. Four vortex ampullae sit 25 mm + 0.35×(AL − 24) from the anterior apex in the ST, SN, IT, and IN quadrants, with that shift capped at ±1.3 mm from the 24 mm eye.

Bubble Sim does not store patient data and should not be used to make treatment or positioning decisions.
